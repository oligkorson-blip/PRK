# Parkwise Brand Voice — Editorial Cheat Sheet

One page. Read it before writing anything a consumer will see. When this sheet and an old screen disagree, this sheet wins.

Parkwise is an illustrative, demo-mode platform. Target returns, occupancy, and income mixes are examples, not offers. Risk language is part of the product — never trim it to make a sentence prettier.

## The four voice attributes

### 1. Calm authority
We sound like someone who has read the deal documents, not someone selling a deal. No urgency, no hype, no legal boilerplate either. State what is true and let the reader decide.

- Prefer: "Browse the numbers, read the details, and decide when you are ready."
- Avoid: "Join thousands of smart investors today."

### 2. Concrete, not clever
Name the real thing the reader can picture. If a sentence needs a second read, rewrite it.

- Prefer: "parking near stations people already use"
- Avoid: "mobility infrastructure assets", "curated real-asset exposure"

### 3. Warm but not chummy
"We" and "you" are welcome. Emojis, slang, and exclamation points are not. Warmth comes from clarity and respect for the reader's time, not from pretending to be a friend.

- Prefer: "We'll email you when the documents are ready."
- Avoid: "You're going to love this one!"

### 4. Risk-literate
Every benefit sits next to its limitation, without apology and without burying it in a footnote. We never imply safety, and we never frame risk language as fine print to rush past.

- Prefer: "Target returns are goals, not promises. Capital at risk."
- Avoid: leading with yield and hiding the risk line below the fold.

## Copy patterns to use

**Benefit + concrete image.** Pair every claim with something the reader can see. "Earn from everyday parking" gets "near the stations, airports, and city centres people already use." Never leave a benefit floating in the abstract.

**What happens next.** After any action, say what follows and when. "Request access" is followed by "We'll review your request and email you an invitation link, valid for 72 hours." Uncertainty reads as evasion; a plain sequence reads as competence.

**No dead-end CTAs.** Every button answers "what do I get when I click?" Use verbs with outcomes: "Browse the catalogue", "Request access", "See the fee breakdown". Not "Submit", "Learn more", or "Get started" when something more specific is available.

**Micro-copy as guidance.** Form hints, empty states, and error messages tell the reader what to do, not just what went wrong. "Enter the email you applied with — we'll send a new link" beats "Invalid input."

## Prohibited words and phrases

Never in consumer copy:

- guaranteed
- passive income
- act now
- unlock
- maximize
- skyrocket
- beat the market
- exclusive deal
- limited time
- hubs (say "opportunities", "places", or "locations")
- advisory phrasing: "recommended for you", "safer", "lower risk"

If a sentence only works with one of these, the sentence is the problem — rewrite the claim, not the rule.

## Before / after

**Hero headline**

- Before: "Unlock exclusive parking investment hubs and maximize your returns."
- After: "They park. You earn. Parking near the stations, airports, and city centres people already use. Capital at risk — target returns are not guaranteed."

**Opportunity card yield line**

- Before: "Earn up to 8% passive income — act now, limited time only!"
- After: "Target return 8% a year, before tax and costs. This is a goal from the deal terms, not a promise — you may receive less, more, later, or nothing."

**Access request confirmation**

- Before: "Success! You're on your way to smarter investing."
- After: "Request received. We'll review it and email your invitation link within two working days. The link is valid for 72 hours."

## Non-negotiables

- Illustrative and capital-at-risk disclaimers stay on every yield, income, and return surface. Never delete, shrink, or soften them.
- Marketing copy lives in `lib/copy/` — do not hard-code consumer strings in components.
- Admin and ops screens may stay precise and operational; this voice is for the public and investor-facing experience.
